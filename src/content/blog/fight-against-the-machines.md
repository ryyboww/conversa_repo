## Abstract

This essay examines the relationship between technological advancement and social awareness. It argues that while technology has expanded humanity's capacity to communicate, produce, and innovate, it has also created new forms of dependence that may weaken the social awareness required for meaningful collective life.

## Introduction

Change is a gradual process, yet societies often attempt to transform themselves without a coherent plan and frequently rely upon ideas whose limitations have already been demonstrated by experience.

Throughout my life, from working a checkout counter at Kmart, cleaning guest rooms at a hotel, serving in the military, working in law enforcement, participating in the hospitality industry, and eventually entering academia, one theme has remained constant: the persistent desire for change. Whether social, political, legal, or institutional, conversations about change appear almost everywhere.

Having been born during a period when distant communication depended upon landlines, radio transmissions, and far less sophisticated technologies than those available today, I understand why modern society has embraced technological advancement. What is more difficult to understand is how technological progress has coincided with a decline in social awareness, community engagement, and meaningful interpersonal connection.

Technology has made communication easier than at any other moment in human history. Yet many individuals appear increasingly disconnected from the people, institutions, and communities that surround them.

This raises an important question: Have we become so focused on building machines that we have neglected the social conditions that make collective life possible?
